## Script

Get comfortable.

You can lie down, or sit somewhere soft and comfortable.

Let your hands rest.

Let your feet relax.

And if you want to close your eyes, you can close them.

There is nothing you have to do right now.

Nobody is going to touch your mouth.

Nobody is going to ask you to do anything.

This is just practice for being calm.

Take a slow breath in through your nose.

And let it out slowly.

Again.

Breathe in gently.

And breathe out slowly.

Imagine that every time you breathe out, your whole body gets just a little heavier.

Your feet get heavy.

Your legs get heavy.

Your tummy gets soft.

Your shoulders drop down.

Your hands become loose.

Your neck gets soft.

Your cheeks get soft.

And your jaw gets loose.

You don't have to hold your teeth together.

Just let your jaw rest.

Feel your tongue resting quietly inside your mouth.

Nothing to change.

Nothing to fix.

Just notice.

Now pay attention to your breathing.

You don't have to take a giant breath.

Just an easy breath in.

And a longer, slower breath out.

In.

And slowly out.

Your body has lots of automatic alarms.

If something gets too close to your eyes, you blink.

If something tickles your nose, you might sneeze.

And your mouth has an alarm too.

Sometimes your mouth says, "Hey, what's that?"

That alarm can feel like a gaggy feeling.

The alarm is trying to protect you.

You don't have to fight it.

You don't have to be scared of it.

You can notice it and say:

"My body knows what to do."

And then breathe out slowly.

Imagine a little wave in the ocean.

The wave comes up.

And then the wave goes back down.

A gaggy feeling can be like that.

It can come up.

And then it can go back down.

You don't have to make it disappear.

You can let it pass.

Now imagine you're sitting in the orthodontist's chair.

Nothing is happening yet.

You're just sitting there.

You are breathing.

You know that you are allowed to take your time.

You know that if you need someone to stop, you can raise your hand.

Imagine raising your hand.

Everything stops.

You take a breath.

And when you are ready, you lower your hand.

You are the boss of your stop signal.

Now imagine someone showing you what they are going to do before they do it.

You look at it.

You understand it.

You take one easy breath.

You decide that you're ready to try one tiny step.

Not the whole thing.

Just one step.

You open your mouth.

You breathe gently through your nose.

Your shoulders stay loose.

Your hands stay soft.

Maybe you notice a funny feeling.

That's okay.

You don't have to panic.

You can think:

"I can handle a funny feeling."

"I can breathe."

"I can pause."

"I am in control."

And then imagine that little wave going back down again.

You did not have to be perfectly calm.

You just practiced being brave while your body felt something new.

That's how your brain learns.

Little by little.

Every time you practice, your brain learns:

"I know this."

"I've done this before."

"I can handle it."

Take one more slow breath in.

And a long, easy breath out.

Feel your legs again.

Feel your hands.

Feel the room around you.

Move your fingers.

Move your toes.

And when you're ready, open your eyes.

You are done.

You don't have to prove anything.

You just practiced.

And every practice makes the next one a little more familiar.
